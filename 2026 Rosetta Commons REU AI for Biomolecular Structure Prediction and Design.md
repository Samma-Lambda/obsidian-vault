###### Personal Statement
I am a major in Mathematics, Data Science, and Computer Science with three years of research experience spanning biophysics, machine learning, operations research, and education, gained through NSF-funded projects and university labs. These experiences have solidified my goal of pursuing a PhD after graduation, ideally in a computationally focused field that is math-heavy but also involves some form of natural science. I am applying to this REU because my home institution does not offer summer research opportunities in computational biology, and I genuinely love to spend my summer conducting research.

I am particularly drawn to research that involves data analysis with complex, high-dimensional data because it typically involves interesting mathematics and requires well-written code to analyze the data in a timely manner.  This is what initially attracted me to machine learning, computer vision, and, more recently, biophysics. Computational protein modeling excites me for similar reasons: I have read about how people model proteins as mathematical knots or graphs, and also read about how people use attention based machine learning to understand proteins. 

My strongest prior experience is in applied machine learning. I was first introduced to Natural Language Processing through a database management course, where we implemented text-based databases and lexeme-level queries. This led to my involvement in an NSF-funded sustainability language project, where I serve as the sole data scientist on a team of researchers with backgrounds in marketing. I curated a large text corpus from sources including Twitter, ESG reports, surveys, and prior studies, and built a machine learning classifier that achieved a 98.5 F1 score for detecting sustainability-related language. I also engineered additional features to improve model performance and designed a pipeline to monitor data drift using information-theoretic divergence measures. This project has required a lot of independent problem solving, learning how to write code that won't be problematic in the future, and learning how to translate marketing needs into actionable data science tasks, and communicate data science findings to non techical members. 

In parallel, I have developed substantial experience in computer vision through both coursework and research. Last semester, I took a computer vision course that became my favorite class of my undergraduate career. I particularly enjoyed learning how Fourier transforms are used in image processing, how chromatic aberrations can reveal image manipulation, and how feature vectors are constructed for learning algorithms. I then applied these ideas in my biophysics research, where I work with a small team studying myosin VI using gliding filament assays. As the only computer scientist on the project, I built tools to analyze filament video data. I modified ATLAS’s convolutional neural networks to improve tracking of dense actin filament networks, developed an auto encoder to mitigate photobleaching effects over time, and applied topological data analysis to identify structural patterns in filament motion under varying ATP concentrations. I have worked on this project largely independently while collaborating closely with biologists and physicists to make sure the software that I create properly meets their needs.

Through these experiences, I have learned that I thrive in collaborative research environments that require strong programming skills, mathematical thinking, and the willingness to learn new scientific domains quickly. Although my formal background in biology is limited, I am comfortable identifying gaps in my knowledge and filling them through focused self-study, a skill that has been essential in my biophysics work. This REU’s focus on computational modeling of protein structures strongly aligns with my interests and skill set. I would love to learn the domain specific mathematics used in this field, and be given a chance to apply my computer science skills in a new setting. 

I am interested in the Ljubetic Lab, the Institute for Protein Design, Tandem AI, the Drew Lab, and the Gray Lab because they all apply machine learning and deep learning, areas in which I have experience and strong interest. The Ljubetic Lab stands out for its work on protein motors, aligning with my current research on Myosin VI. Tandem AI for its multidisciplinary, collaborative environment.
