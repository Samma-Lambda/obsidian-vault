# Ring Definition and Axioms
A ring is the next natural step in algebraic structures. Under addition it is considered a group, but it is also equipped with another binary operation denoted by $\cdot$ or multiplication. Here are the axioms of a ring 
- It is a group under addition $+$
- It is associative under multiplication $(ab)c=a(bc)$
- Multiplication is distributed over addition IE $a(b+c)=ab+ac$
It works similarly to groups where a subring is just a ring contained within a ring. 

# Zero Divisors and Intergral Domains
A zero divisor is an element of a ring $x$ such that there exists another element $y$ such that $xy=0$. An example of this would be $[2]\in \mathbb{Z}/4$ because $[2]\cdot [2]=[0]$. A ring with no zero divisors is known as a Integral domain  

# Ideals and Quotients Rings
An ideal is the equivalent of a normal subgroup but for rings. The conditions for being an ideal of a ring is 
1. An ideal is closed under addition 
2. $\forall x \in I$ and $\forall r \in R$ we find that $rx\in I$
3. $\forall x \in I$ and $\exists -x \in I$
This allows us to create a quotient ring allowing us to have a ring of $\mathbb{Z}/5$
