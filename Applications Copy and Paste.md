I am particularly interested in research that involves both theory heavy mathematics and computer science. More specifically, I am drawn to probability and data analysis techniques such as Markov chain Monte Carlo methods and topological data analysis. These interests have led me to pursue research in areas including computer vision, natural language processing, and operations research.

In my natural language processing research, I curated a large corpus of text from Twitter, ESG reports, surveys, and prior studies and trained a machine learning classifier that achieved a 98.5 F1 score in detecting sustainable language. My work included building the classifier, engineering additional features to improve performance, and developing a pipeline to monitor data drift using informational divergence. I particularly enjoyed reading about and experimenting with statistical methods for identifying significant n grams across corpora.

In parallel, I have conducted biophysics research with a small team of biologists and physicists studying myosin VI using gliding filament assays. As the sole computer scientist on the project, I developed tools to analyze filament video data, which required adapting convolutional neural networks to track dense actin filament networks. I also built an autoencoder to address photobleaching effects over time and applied topological data analysis to study structural patterns in filament motion under different ATP concentrations.

I have also worked in operations research through the Computational Applied Mathematics Program REU at California Polytechnic State University. In this program, I studied linear programming for truck and drone delivery scheduling. I developed the team’s mixed integer linear programming model, analyzed optimization constraints, and helped evaluate algorithmic tradeoffs. This work resulted in a 30 page technical report that I prepared in LaTeX.

# Personal Experience
Besides my coursework, my experiences in public speaking, organizing groups, and technical communication have prepared me to be an effective contributor to the SULI Program. Through my roles as a computer science TA, research presenter, and tutor, I have developed skills that I am eager to apply in a collaborative research setting.

Over the past year, I have made significant progress in public speaking. When I first became a TA, I was interested in teaching but intimidated by speaking in front of a class. After talking with my professor, he offered me the opportunity to give a few lectures to see if I enjoyed it. I presented on hash functions and Bloom filters; the first lecture was rough, but I quickly found my rhythm. Since then, I have given seven lectures, each one improving on the last. I am no longer nervous in front of a crowd and have developed better techniques for preparing and delivering lectures. Beyond teaching, I have presented my research at multiple math conferences in talks ranging from 15 minutes to an hour. These experiences taught me how to compress complex material while keeping presentations engaging(though it is still painful to see ten weeks of research condensed into 15 minutes).

I also have extensive experience organizing and coordinating groups. As an active member of my university’s math club, I helped plan trips to conferences such as JMM and NUMS. As a TA, I organized study groups for midterms and finals in courses like Analysis of Algorithms and Intro to Data Science, helping peers navigate challenging material efficiently. These experiences taught me how to schedule times that work for everyone, balance different needs, and use tools like Google surveys to ensure all voices are heard.

Finally, my technical communication skills have been strengthened through both teaching and tutoring. I regularly explain complex concepts, ranging from p-values and mathematical proofs to programming techniques like operator overloading, in ways that are clear and approachable. I am eager to bring these skills to the SULI Program and continue developing them. I genuinely love math and computer science and would be grateful for the opportunity to contribute.
