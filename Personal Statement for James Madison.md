This is the first REU I’m applying to this year because it involves topology (I actually found it by searching for the keyword “topology” on the Steve Butler REU page). While I enjoy many areas of mathematics, over the past year I’ve developed a particular passion for topology, both as a theoretical subject and as a tool for understanding data.

My interest began last summer when a researcher visited and gave a talk on topological data analysis (TDA). I was immediately hooked, as the talk combined both rigorous mathematics and data analysis, two areas I’m very interested in. After the talk had concluded, I approached them and asked how they would recommend learning TDA. They told me that it was incredibly important to build a strong theoretical foundation in topology before looking into TDA, and pointed me towards Professor Staekers' point set topology lectures and algebraic topology lectures from Andrews University. 

That summer, I began working through these lectures, averaging about one a day. These topics were initially confusing (especially cohomology), but they genuinely put a smile on my face, so I was able to push through. Some of my best memories from last summer are reading about topology in the sun. 

I will be the first to admit that while I found these video lectures interesting, I had learned these topics as a tourist, not building a rigorous understanding. To rectify this, I signed up for a topology class. This ended up being my favorite math class I had ever taken. To this day, my favorite proof remains the classification of compact, connected, triangulated 2-manifolds. I like the way it breaks down into 5 nice cases in the inductive hypothesis.    

I’ve also enjoyed applying topology in a hands-on research setting. This last semester, I joined a biophysics group studying gliding filament assays for myosin VI, where I analyzed recorded filament motion. This gave me the opportunity to apply TDA to video data. Using cubical persistence with the Python GUDHI package, we found that different ATP concentrations led to filament configurations with noticeably different void sizes.

While I have been able to conduct research in the past, I am reaching out to other schools due to the fact that my school is a low research institution, and there are no math research programs at my school.  Joining this program will not only expand topology skills, but it will also help me achieve my goal of pursuing a PhD in some form of computational mathematics. 

While I would love to be a part of every single project within this program, I am my top choices would be Persistent Homology of Metric Graph Families due to the involvement of programming in python, or Representation Varieties and the Topology of Three-Manifolds due to the involvement of Abstract Algebra which is my second favorite class, and involvement with varieties which we talked about in my Abstract Algebra 3 class but I am incredibly interested in learning more about.
