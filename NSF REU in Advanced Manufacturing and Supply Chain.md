##### Personal Statement 
I am currently studying Computer Science, Data Science, and Mathematics at Willamette University. The combination of these fields, along with my previous research on last-mile delivery, makes me a strong candidate for this REU.

My interest in logistics research began last summer when I participated in the CAMP REU at California Polytechnic University. In this program, I studied last-mile truck and drone delivery using linear programming, focused on scheduling models that coordinate multiple vehicle types and customer constraints. My responsibilities included developing the team’s mixed-integer linear programming model, evaluating and refining optimization constraints, coauthoring a 30-page technical report, and conducting sensitivity analyses on our model.

This past semester, I expanded my skills in logistics by developing a prototype app designed to help volunteers transport goods between food banks during their daily commutes. The idea came from my time volunteering at a food bank, where I saw both surplus and shortage of resources. Food would expire at one site while another location urgently needed it. The problem was not a lack of resources but a lack of coordination/manpower. To address this, I designed a simulated annealing heuristic that minimizes deviation from users’ normal commutes while allowing them to incorporate pickup and drop-off points. I chose to use a heuristic rather than an exact solution prevents large wait time that would discourage users. I am currently still developing this project with my professor.  

Beyond logistics and operations research, I also have a strong background in data science. I have taken courses in Database Management, Machine Learning, Inferential Statistics, Stochastic Processes, and Probability, and I have been a TA for Intro to Data Science. I also worked as a data scientist on an NSF funded natural language processing project. My contributions included building a machine learning sentence classifier, engineering features to improve performance, and creating a pipeline to monitor data drift using informational divergence. Working in this role taught me how to apply data science in a real setting, communicate technical ideas to nontechnical teammates, design flexible and maintainable databases, and pick up new analysis methods quickly.

I would be grateful for the chance to contribute to this program. Because I attend a low research institution, I cannot study this field at my school, and this REU would be a key step toward my goal of earning a PhD in Operations Research or Industrial Engineering.
