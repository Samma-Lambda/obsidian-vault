By the end of the semester, things had completely turned around. I pulled my grades up to all A’s and felt genuinely excited about the classes I would take next. I ended up joining my school's Math Club and Computer Science Club. I even registered into my first hackthon(which I got an award in!). I’ve kept the study habits I built during that time and now make a point of studying a little bit every day for each class.

Now I have found myself completely committed to academia. I work as both a TA and a Tutor, and often talk to struggling students about the changes I made to succeed academically. I love all my classes, and often I find myself asking the most questions. In a strange way, I am grateful for this challenge because it helped me discover that I enjoy engaging deeply with classes.

##### Please describe one or more times in your life in which you needed to exercise leadership
I’ve had a few leadership roles over the years, starting in high school as the president of my school’s film club and as the organizer of a Dungeons & Dragons club at my local library. While those were great experiences, I really grew as a leader during my second year of college when I became both a tutor and a teaching assistant.

I worked as a tutor in my university’s QUAD center, helping students with math, computer science, and data science, and I was also the only TA for an introductory data science course with about fifty students. Most of the students were completely new to programming, so things got overwhelming quickly, especially during lectures when I had to help debug code on the fly. After a rough start, I figured out a system that worked. I put together a short list of common bugs and quick fixes and distributed that to the class. I met regularly with the professor to understand where students were getting stuck. That structure made a big difference and helped me support students more effectively.

The next semester pushed me in a different way. While TAing for Data Structures, I was asked to run lectures for a week while the professor was at a conference. My first lecture didn’t go as planned. I had trouble getting the class to settle down, and it took longer than I expected to get started. Afterward, I asked other professors for advice and adjusted how I ran class. I started being more assertive, clearly outlining the plan for each lecture, and giving a motivating example at the beginning. The next lectures went much more smoothly, and I’ve since had more opportunities to lecture.

These experiences massively improved my leadership skills, but also showed me I have a lot of areas I need to grow in. They also helped me realize how much I enjoy teaching and leading in academic settings, and they’ve made me excited about becoming a professor in the future.
