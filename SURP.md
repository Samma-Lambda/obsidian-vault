###### 1. Why are you interested in research at the interface of mathematics and biology, and this program in particular? Why should you be chosen for this program? (200-500 words)
Since starting college, my academic interests have been mostly in mathematics. More recently, however, I have become interested in biology through my experience as a researcher in a biophysics lab. In this lab, we study myosin VI microscopy data, which involves both lab work to collect data and programming to analyze said data. This has been my favorite research project to date, largely because I get to apply my math skills in an interesting setting while working with physicists and biologists.

I entered this project with minimal formal biology background. Prior to this, my research experience was primarily in more mathematical fields such as operations research and natural language processing. In these research projects, I either worked largely independently or within teams composed of mathematicians. The biophysics project was massively different. From the start, I was working in the lab, collaborating with physicists to model forces and dynamics within our simulations, and working with biologists to determine what questions were most meaningful to ask about myosin VI. This forced me to quickly learn basic domain knowledge in this field and learn how to effectively communicate with non-mathematicians in research.

I found this incredibly rewarding. It was satisfying to see my math skills used not just to prove theorems, but to better understand physical systems and design experiments. While I wish to remain focused on mathematics in the future, I also wish to continue to branch into fields involving the natural sciences. In particular, I wish to one day pursue a PhD in either biophysics or computational biology. Being able to join this program would be a massive step toward achieving this goal and would give me further clarity on which fields I am specifically interested in.

I believe I would be a strong fit for this program due to my solid background in mathematics, programming, and data analysis, as well as my ability to work effectively across different domains. Having conducted research in both NLP and biophysics, I am comfortable acquiring domain-specific knowledge through literature and collaboration. I have also developed strong communication skills, frequently explaining advanced data analysis and topological data analysis techniques to collaborators without technical backgrounds. I would be incredibly happy to bring my skills to this program and further develop myself as a scientist.

###### 3. What challenges have you overcome to get to where you are now? How have these challenges impacted you as a person, scholar or researcher? (200-500 words)
I entered college without the academic habits needed to succeed. In high school, I didn't really study and often was distracted in class. This was not because I was uninterested, I genuinely enjoyed what I was learning in school. However, because I could get good grades with minimal effort, I never learned how to study effectively. 

My first semester of college was a sharp contrast to high school. Courses were way more challenging, and demanded way more effort. When I got my first midterm exams back and saw that my highest score was a C+, it was a clear wake-up call. 

Over the remainder of that semester, I changed my approach to learning. I spent a massive amount of time in the tutoring center, watched youtube videos to fill in gaps, and began actually reading the supplemental texts. Additionally, I began studying on a daily basis rather than the night before the exam. Once I had made these changes, my relationship with learning. Lectures made sense while they were being given, concepts started to become more intuitive, and I found myself understanding the underlying ideas rather than memorizing a procedure to get the correct answer.

By the end of the semester, my grades had improved to all A’s. But more importantly, I actually felt excited about my education. I joined my schools Math Club and Computer Science Club and registered for my first hackathon(Which I got an award in!). The study habits I developed this semester have stuck with me since, and now I make a point to study each class I am in a little bit each day. 

This challenge changed me both academically and personally. It instilled a strong commitment to learning and made me more empathetic toward struggling peers. As a TA and tutor, I often share my experience with students who feel overwhelmed, helping them develop the same habits that let me succeed. In hindsight, I am grateful for this challenge, it taught me how to properly learn and showed me that I enjoy school.
