#### If you have participated in scientific research, what did you like about it? If you don’t have any research experience, tell us what you liked about another work experience or favorite class.
I have participated in research in Education, Operations Research, Biophysics, and Data Science, and I have enjoyed being involved in each area. I like many aspects of research, but what I enjoy most is spitballing solutions as a group, working with a team, and attending scientific conferences.

I love when the whole group comes together to brainstorm, either at the beginning of a project, or when someone encounters a more difficult challenge and needs help. Its so fun to write everything out on a whiteboard and have everyone shout out their ideas. Its very satisfying to see ideas snowball into solutions. Strangely this process always reminds me of improv because everyone is "yes and"-ing each others ideas. 

Beyond brainstorming as a group, I also love working in a group in a more general sense. I especially love working in groups where everyone has different backgrounds. Recently I have been collaborating with biologists and physicists in my biophysics research, and have been loving every second of it. Its nice being able to consult the biologists to figure out how to make my simulations more accurate, and being able to work with physicists to figure out insane change of coordinates or conversions between weird units. I think that you learn the most when working in an interdisciplinary group, and the new perspective that you gain will be invaluable. I will always be grateful to a physicist who gave me a fresh perspective on tensors, which finally allowed me to understand them.

Thirdly, I enjoy attending scientific conferences. I’ve loved popular science since I was about nine, and conferences feel like being inside a one of the magazines. I like hearing people passionately explain their research, and I’ve found that these environments to be so uplifting and supportive. Every-time I leave one of these conferences I am always so inspired and motivated. 

You might wonder why I don’t just work in industry, where I could still collaborate with a team and attend science conferences on the side. For me, the difference is completing a milestone in a research project. There’s something really satisfying about looking back and seeing what the team has accomplished. 


#### If you think you might be interested in a career in research, why? If not research, tell us about the career that interests you.
I’m very interested in a career in research. I have been interested in science for as long as I can remember, and college has only deepened this interest by giving me the opportunity to engage more. Learning to program was a pivotal moment for me because it allowed me to start working on my own personal projects. I am the type of person who always needs a project, so I started by writing quirky cellular automata. As my math and programming skills improved, I began to work on more ambitious projects, such as my MCMC analysis of cosmological constants (I reached out to a professor for guidance on this project) or my quantum computer simulations.

Additionally, I think it would be way more satisfying to make or discover something new, rather than working for a company and shipping a new project.

My current plans are to pursue a PhD of some form. I know I want to do something that is math heavy, but I find everything I run across so interesting, I have had a hard time narrowing it down more than that. My current pipe dream is to host an REU of my own one day. 



#### Describe how you've dealt with an academic challenge and how you worked through it. (For example, if your GPA is lower than you would like, this is a chance to explain.).
I didn’t really study in high school and spent a lot of time zoning out in class. It wasn’t that I didn’t care. I actually found many of the subjects interesting. I just didn’t need to pay much attention or put in much effort to do well, so I never developed strong study habits.

College was a completely different experience. My first semester was hard, and I quickly realized I didn’t know how to support myself academically. I remember getting my first round of midterms back and seeing that my highest grade was a C+. That was a clear wake-up call.

For the rest of that semester, I began learning how to study effectively. I spent a lot of time at the tutoring center, watched YouTube videos to fill in gaps, and began reading the optional supplemental texts. Once I started doing that, I realized I enjoyed school much more. Lectures made sense while I was in them, not just the night before an exam, and I began to understand concepts intuitively instead of just memorizing steps to get the right answer.

By the end of the semester, things had completely turned around. I pulled my grades up to all A’s and felt genuinely excited about the classes I would take next. I ended up joining my school's Math Club and Computer Science Club. I even registered into my first hackthon(which I got an award in!). I’ve kept the study habits I built during that time and now make a point of studying a little bit every day for each class.

Now I have found myself completely committed to academia. I work as both a TA and a Tutor, and often talk to struggling students about the changes I made to succeed academically. I love all my classes, and often I find myself asking the most questions. In a strange way, I am grateful for this challenge because it helped me discover that I enjoy engaging deeply with classes.
