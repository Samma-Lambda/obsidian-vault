#### Personal Statement
I am a third year student at Willamette University pursuing degrees in Computer Science, Data Science, and Mathematics. After completing my undergraduate studies, I plan to pursue a PhD in quantum algorithms, probabilistic algorithms, or data science. Because Willamette is a low research institution, I am applying to this program both to gain the research experience needed to be a competitive graduate school applicant and because I would genuinely enjoy spending a summer doing math research.

I am particularly interested in research that involves both theory heavy mathematics and computer science. More specifically, I am drawn to fields involving probability and data analysis techniques such as Markov chain Monte Carlo and topological data analysis. These interests have led me to pursue research in areas including computer vision, natural language processing, and operations research.

In a NSF funded research project in natural language processing, I curated a large corpus of text from Twitter, ESG reports, surveys, and prior studies and trained a machine learning classifier that achieved a 98.5 F1 score in detecting sustainable language. My work included building the classifier, engineering additional features to improve performance, and developing a pipeline to monitor data drift using informational divergence. I particularly enjoyed reading about and experimenting with statistical methods for identifying significant n grams across corpora.

In parallel, I have conducted biophysics research with a small team of biologists and physicists studying myosin VI using gliding filament assays. As the sole computer scientist on the project, I developed tools to analyze filament video data, which required adapting convolutional neural networks to track dense actin filament networks. I also built an autoencoder to address photobleaching effects over time and applied topological data analysis to study structural patterns in filament motion under different ATP concentrations.

I have also worked in operations research through the Computational Applied Mathematics Program REU at California Polytechnic State University. In this program, I studied linear programming for truck and drone delivery scheduling. I developed the team’s mixed integer linear programming model, analyzed optimization constraints, and helped evaluate algorithmic tradeoffs. This work resulted in a 30 page technical report that I prepared in LaTeX. I particularly the graph theory and linear algebra that was involved in this project. 

Beyond my research experiences, I have challenged myself academically by taking a large number of upper level courses. Classes such as Probability and Computing, Mathematical Statistics, and Topology were especially influential and strengthened my interest in mathematically grounded data analysis. With what I learned from these courses, I have created a few personal projects in my free time. These include a simulation of quantum algorithms using NumPy matrices, an implementation of Elliptic Curve cryptography, and a program which writes linear recursive sequences as a polynomial automatically. All of these projects are linked on my resume. 

Overall, through both my research experiences and coursework, I have learned that I enjoy working on math heavy programming projects in collaborative research settings. I like problems that require careful reasoning, experimentation, and discussion, and I tend to do my best work when I am part of a group that is actively thinking through ideas together. Outside of academics, I enjoy designing board games, and doing improv, both of which reflect how I like to work and think. Board game design pushes me to think carefully about structure and emergent behavior, while improv has helped me be more comfortable communicating ideas clearly and adapting in a group setting. 

Thank you for your time and consideration, I would appreciate any opportunity to do math this summer
