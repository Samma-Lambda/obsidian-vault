##### Personal Statement 
I’m a Mathematics, Data Science, and Computer Science major, and over the past three years, I’ve gotten the chance to research in biophysics, machine learning, operations research, and education through NSF-funded projects and university labs. These experiences have made me really excited about the idea of pursuing a PhD after graduation. I’m applying to REUs like this one because my school doesn’t offer summer research in these areas, and I want to spend the summer studying the topics that I love.

I am particularly interested in Natural Language Processing and Computer Vision as areas of research. These topics feel magical to me, I love that we can look at an image of a snake and know that it is a snake, but not know why its a snake. Similarly with language, we can tell that a word feels different in a different context, but are not able to quantify why. I also love how the complex data in these fields requires both clever coding to make sure that the algorithm can run in a timely manner, and advanced mathematics to interpret the high dimensional data. 

I first got introduced to basic Natural Language Processing within the context of a database management class. Within this class, we discussed and implemented various text-based databases and did some basic lexeme based queries. A marketing professor at my school's sister school, PNCA, reached out to my school looking for someone with NLP skills. My professor recommended me for the job, and I soon excitedly joined the team. We have been researching the application of sustainable language within machine learning. All the other members of the team have PhDs in marketing and do not have a large amount of mathematical or programming knowledge, so I have been the sole data scientist.

Within this project, I curated a large corpus of text from sources like Twitter, ESG reports, surveys, and previous studies. I built a machine learning classifier that achieved a 98.5 F1 score in detecting sustainable language, engineered additional features to improve performance, and even designed a pipeline to monitor data drift using informational divergence. I have been working on this project part time for about a year and a half now, but it is only part-time and is going to break for the summer. 

Aside from NLP, I also have experience with computer vision. This last semester, I got an opportunity to take a course on the subject. This was my favorite course I have taken in my entire undergraduate career. I liked learning how Fourier transforms are used in image processing, how chromatic aberrations can be used to detect photoshopped images, and the process of creating feature vectors for machine learning classifiers. 

I also have experience working with computer vision, in the biophysics research lab I am involved in. I work with a small team of biologists and physicists studying myosin VI using gliding filament assays. I am the only computer scientist on the team, so I spend most of my time building tools to make sense of the filament video data. I modified ATLAS’s convolutional neural networks to more effectively track dense actin filament networks and developed an auto-encoder to reduce the effects of photobleaching over time. I also applied topological data analysis to uncover structural patterns in filament motion, which helped us identify distinct void-size distributions under different ATP concentrations. I’ve been working on this project for about six months, mostly independently, but I regularly consult with teammates and my computer vision professor when I need guidance. 

All of these experiences have made it clear that I really enjoy programming, exploring domain-specific mathematics, and being part of a collaborative research environment. Participating in this REU would be an amazing opportunity to continue exploring research I’m passionate about. Both the NLP project and the Biophysics project I am involved in do not continue over the summer. I am hoping to join this project as a way to continue researching, and be able to gain the experience nessacary to be a competitive candidate when applying to graduate school to hopefully study these topics.
