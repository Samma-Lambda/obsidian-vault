##### Briefly describe why you are interested in the topics of the Undergraduate Summer School:
This past semester I had the opportunity to take a topology course, which ended up being one of my favorite math classes. The course started with point-set topology, then moved into algebraic topology, and ended with a unit on knot theory.

While I enjoyed point-set topology, I absolutely LOVED algebraic topology. I already enjoyed abstract algebra, and getting to use it in a new setting was exciting. I enjoyed how difficult it was to rigorously prove things that seem obvious, such as the isomorphism between the fundamental group of the circle and the integers. The course significantly expanded my mathematical abilities, and I got way better at using functions to map spaces into other spaces to prove traits about the original space.

I also really enjoyed the knot theory portion of the class, but for different reasons. I loved seeing all different fields of math used to define knot invariants. I was mind blown to learn that knots can have determinants. Due to time constraints, the course could not go very far into these topics, only reaching a proof of Alexander’s theorem. When I asked my professor how I could continue engaging with knot theory, she recommended that I apply to REUs in this area, which is how I found this program.

Overall, I am hoping to join this program to continue learning about knot theory. I really enjoyed the subject and would love the chance to improve my understanding of it. 


##### What are your current or potential mathematical interests? Are your interests more theoretical or applied?
While I find nearly all areas of mathematics interesting, I am find myself enjoying abstract algebra, topology, and number theory the most.

I enjoy these fields because results that are true are often unintuitive, while results are intuitive  can feel near impossible to prove. Some of the hardest and most satisfying proofs I have worked through were for seemingly simple results, such as proving that R2 is normal.

I tend to prefer theoretical mathematics, but I also appreciate seeing these fields applied. In my current biophysics research, I use topological data analysis to study filament networks, and in my free time I enjoy learning about cryptography, often implementing cryptographic algorithms from scratch. I am most excited by theory-heavy applied areas of mathematics, though I am equally happy with purely theoretical work. I believe that mathematics for its own sake is beautiful, and even when it has no immediate application, the skills developed by studying it are extremely valuable. I think of it like doing pushups: you will never be asked to do one in the real world, but the strength you build still matters.
